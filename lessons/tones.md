## English Syllables Have Stress

The English word _desert_ has two pronunciations, one with the
stress on the first syllable, the other with the stress on the second
syllable.  If you put the stress on the incorrect syllable, you can
cause confusion or lack of understanding.

Listen to how the following sentence is pronounced, and think about
how you feel after hearing it.

<egsentence src="swapdesert">The soldier deserted his post in the desert.</egsentence>

Every English word with more than one syllable has at least one
stressed syllable.  Whenever you learn a new word and its
pronunciation, one thing you have to learn is which syllables are
stressed.

## Chinese Syllables Have Tone

Chinese syllables have _tones_.  Each time you pronounce a
syllable, your voice has to begin at a certain relative pitch;
depending on the tone, your pitch will go up, go down, stay at the
same level, or maybe even go down and come back up by the time you get
to the end of the syllable.

In English, only some syllables of a word are stressed.  In Chinese,
except for a few special cases, **every syllable of every word has a
tone**.  It is as important to learn the tones in a Chinese word as
it is to learn the stressed syllables in an English word.

## What the Tones Sound Like

Chinese has four tones.  To get an idea of what they sound like,
consider the following dialogue.

<egsentence src="yougo">"You go!"</egsentence>
<egsentence src="whome">"Who, me?"</egsentence>
<egsentence src="yesyou">"Yes, you!"</egsentence>

The _first tone_ is high and level, like the word <eg
src="dialogue-you">You</eg> in "You go!"  It is marked with a straight
line over the main vowel, like <py>ā</py>.

Click on the following buttons to hear the syllables in the first
tone.

<listen syl="dū tiāo piān chēng"></listen>

The _second tone_ starts in the middle of the voice range and
goes straight up, like the word <eg src="dialogue-me">me</eg> in "Who,
me?"  It is marked with a rising line over the main vowel, like
<py>á</py>.

Click on the following buttons to hear the syllables in the second
tone.

<listen syl="dú tiáo pián chéng"></listen>

The _third tone_ starts near the bottom of the voice range and
dips even lower before coming back up a little.  It's like the
sentence <eg src="whome">Who, me?</eg>, but it doesn't come up so high
at the end.  It is marked with a "v" over the main vowel, like
<py>ǎ</py>.

Click on the following buttons to hear the syllables in the third
tone.

<listen syl="dǔ tiǎo piǎn chěng"></listen>

The _fourth tone_ starts at the top of the voice range and drops
quickly to the bottom, like the word <eg src="dialogue-yes">Yes</eg>
in "Yes, you!"  It is marked with a falling line over the main vowel,
like <py>à</py>.

Click on the following buttons to hear the syllables in the fourth
tone.

<listen syl="dù tiào piàn chèng"></listen>
