## Explanation

The initial <py>ch</py> sounds like the _ch_ sound in the English
word <eg src="char">char</eg>.  It is like a _pinyin_ <py>t</py> followed by
a _pinyin_ <py>sh</py>.

The initial <py>zh</py> may _sound_ like the _j_ in the
English word <eg src="jar">jar</eg>, but it isn't.  If you put your finger on
your Adam's apple and say <eg src="jar">jar</eg>, you will feel it vibrate when
you say the _j_ sound.  <py>zh</py> doesn't vibrate.  It is like
a _pinyin_ <py>d</py> followed by a _pinyin_ <py>sh</py>.

The difference between <py>zh</py> and <py>ch</py> is exactly the
difference between <py>z</py> and <py>c</py>: <py>c</py> and
<py>ch</py> have a puff of air after them, while <py>z</py> and
<py>zh</py> don't.

final <egpy>a</egpy>.

**Note:** The initials <py>zh</py> and <py>ch</py> never combine
with finals beginning with <py>y</py>.
