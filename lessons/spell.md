## Introduction

Before looking at the last three initials, we're going to have a
lesson on spelling rules.

_Pinyin_ is not always spelled perfectly phonetically.  As we've
see before, sometimes there is an extra letter that is silent.  At
other times, there is a letter missing that you think should be
there.  We will look at the "missing letter" rules and mention the
reasons for them.

## Spelling Rules (1)

**When** <egpy>wen</egpy> **occurs after initials,** <py>w</py>
**becomes** <py>u</py> **and the** <py>e</py> **is dropped.
The tone mark goes over the** <py>u</py>**.**

Click on the buttons to hear the pronunciations.  Notice how
<py>un</py> almost sounds like two syllables pushed together, a
<egpy>wu</egpy> sound and a short <egpy>en</egpy> sound.

<listen syl="cǔn kùn"></listen>

**Reason:** For some speakers, the <py>e</py> sound is very weak in
the first and second tones after most initials.  Thus, the committee
decided to remove the <py>e</py> from the spelling altogether.

## Spelling Rules (2)

**When** <egpy>you</egpy> **occurs after initials,** <py>y</py>
**becomes** <py>i</py> **and the** <py>o</py> **is dropped.
The tone mark goes over the** <py>u</py>**.**

Click on the buttons to hear the pronunciations.  Notice how an
<py>o</py> sound sneaks in between the <py>i</py> and the <py>u</py>.

<listen syl="liù diū"></listen>

**Reason:**  For some speakers, the <py>o</py> sound is very weak
in the first and second tones.  Thus, the committee decided to remove
the <py>o</py> from the spelling altogether.

**When** <egpy>wei</egpy> **occurs after initials,** <py>w</py>
**becomes** <py>u</py> **and the** <py>e</py> **is dropped.
The tone mark goes over the** <py>i</py>**.**

Click on the buttons to hear the pronunciations.  Notice how an
<py>e</py> sound sneaks in between the <py>u</py> and the <py>i</py>.

<listen syl="guǐ tuī"></listen>

**Reason:** For some speakers, the <py>e</py> sound is very weak in
the first and second tones after most initials.  Thus, the committee
decided to remove the <py>e</py> from the spelling altogether.

## Spelling Rules (3)

**After** <py>b p m f</py>**,** <egpy>wo</egpy> **is
spelled** <py>o</py>**.**

Click on the following buttons to hear the pronunciations.  Notice how
a <py>u</py> sound definitely sneaks in.

<listen syl="bō pò mǒ fó"></listen>

**Reason:**  When the pronunciation of Mandarin was standardized in
the 1920s to more closely follow the pronunciation of Beijing, it was
decided not to mark this <py>u</py> sound.  (In fact, most
romanization schemes leave it out.)
