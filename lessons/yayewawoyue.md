## Explanation

In this lesson, we combine the <py>y</py>, <py>w</py>, and <py>yu</py>
sounds with the three vowels <py>a e o</py>.  Note that not every
combination is possible; for instance, there is no <py>we</py>.

The syllables <py>ya</py> and <py>wa</py> sound pretty much like you
might guess.  Click on the buttons below to hear <py>ya</py> and
<py>wa</py> in the four tones.

<tonerows rawsyl="ya wa"></tonerows>

The syllables <py>ye</py> and <py>yue</py> rhyme.  The only difference
is that you round your lips as for <py>wu</py> for the second one.
Click on the buttons below to hear <py>ye</py> and <py>yue</py> in the
four tones.

<tonerows rawsyl="ye yue"></tonerows>

Notice that <py>ye</py> doesn't sound like the English words
<eg src="yea">yea</eg> or <eg src="yay">yay</eg>.  It rhymes with the English word
<eg src="eh">eh</eg>, which you might use if you are Canadian or hard of
hearing.  The same goes for <py>yue</py>.

Note also that <egpy>ye</egpy> and <egpy>yue</egpy> do _not_
rhyme with <egpy>e</egpy>.  (Click to hear the difference.)  The
letter <py>e</py> has _two_ pronunciations, depending on what
letters are around it.  <py>e</py> is pronounced like <egpy>e</egpy>
when it is alone or when it is not next to a vowel or the letter
<py>y</py>; otherwise, it sounds like <eg src="ehat">e</eg>.

Click on the buttons below to hear the syllable <py>wo</py> in the
four tones.

<tonerows rawsyl="wo"></tonerows>

Notice that <egpy>wo</egpy> doesn't sound like the English words
<eg src="woe">woe</eg> or <eg src="whoa">whoa</eg>.  When you say <eg src="whoa">whoa</eg>, your lips
start close together, come apart for the _o_ sound, and then come
back together.  When you say <egpy>wo</egpy>, your lips spread apart
but never come back together.
