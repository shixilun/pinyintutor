## Explanation

Some _pinyin_ syllables, instead of ending with a vowel, end in
<py>n</py> or <py>ng</py>.

The syllables <py>an</py> and <py>ang</py> are just the sound
<egpy>a</egpy> followed by the sound <py>n</py> or <py>ng</py>.

Click on the buttons to hear <py>an</py> and <py>ang</py> in the four
tones.

<tonerows rawsyl="an ang"></tonerows>

**Note:** <egpy>ang</egpy> does _not_ rhyme with the English
word <eg src="bang">bang</eg>.

The syllable <py>ong</py> is just the sound <egpy>o</egpy> followed by
the sound <py>ng</py>.

Click on the buttons to hear <py>ong</py> in the four tones.

<tonerows rawsyl="ong"></tonerows>

**Note:** There is no _on_ in _pinyin_; if you think you
hear _on_, you probably hear <egpy>an</egpy>.

**Note:** <egpy>ong</egpy> does _not_ rhyme with the English
word <eg src="gong">gong</eg>.  If you hear something that rhymes with
<eg src="gong">gong</eg>, you probably hear <egpy>ang</egpy>.

The syllables <py>en</py> and <py>eng</py> are just the sound
<egpy>e</egpy> followed by the sound <py>n</py> or <py>ng</py>.  (Note
that <egpy>e</egpy> has the pronunciation that it does because it is
not next to a vowel or <py>y</py>.)

Click on the buttons to hear <py>en</py> and <py>eng</py> in the four
tones.

<tonerows rawsyl="en eng"></tonerows>

**Note:** There is no _ung_ in _pinyin_; if you think you
hear _ung_, you probably hear <egpy>eng</egpy>.

**Note:** There _is_ a <py>un</py> in _pinyin_, but it
has a completely different sound, and we won't see it for quite some
time.  So, if you think you hear _un_, you probably hear
<egpy>en</egpy>.
