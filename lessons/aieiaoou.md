## Explanation (1)

In this lesson, we consider the four two-vowel combinations of
_pinyin_.  They are not too difficult to pronounce; however,
their spelling can be somewhat tricky, and it's easy to be confused
about which combination has which pronunciation.

First, we consider <py>ai</py> and <py>ei</py>.  <egpy>ai</egpy> is
the combination of the <egpy>a</egpy> sound and the <egpy>yi</egpy>
sound.  However, <egpy>ei</egpy> is _not_ the combination of the
<egpy>e</egpy> sound and the <egpy>yi</egpy> sound.  Since this
<py>e</py> is next to a vowel, <egpy>ei</egpy> sounds like the
combination of <eg src="ehat">e</eg> and <egpy>yi</egpy>.

Click on the buttons to hear <py>ai</py> and <py>ei</py> in the four
tones.

<tonerows rawsyl="ai ei"></tonerows>

Here are some things some students find confusing:

1. No _pinyin_ syllable ever ends with <py>y</py>.  Thus, you
should never write these syllables as <py>ay</py> or <py>ey</py>.

2. Because <py>ai</py> has the letter <py>a</py> in it, there is a
tendency to write <py>ai</py> (or <py>ay</py>) when you hear
<egpy>ei</egpy>.

3. Because <egpy>ai</egpy> sounds like the letter I, there is a
tendency to use English spellings.  For instance, you might want to
write <egpy>dai</egpy> as _die_ or <egpy>bai</egpy> as
_bye_.  Remember that in _pinyin_, syllables that rhyme
should be spelled the same.

## Explanation (2)

Next, consider <py>ao</py> and <py>ou</py>.  <egpy>ao</egpy> is the
combination of the <egpy>a</egpy> sound and the <egpy>wu</egpy> sound.
Some people think it should have been written as <py>au</py>; however,
it was decided that in handwriting, <py>au</py> and <py>an</py> would
be easily confused, so <py>ao</py> was chosen instead.

<egpy>ou</egpy> is the combination of the <egpy>o</egpy> and the
<egpy>wu</egpy> sound.  In particular, <egpy>o</egpy> and
<egpy>ou</egpy> do _not_ sound the same.

Click on the buttons to hear <py>ao</py> and <py>ou</py> in the four
tones.

Because in English the _ow_ combination can sound like
<egpy>ao</egpy> or <egpy>ou</egpy>, there is the temptation to use
_ow_ in your spelling.  No syllable in _pinyin_ ever ends
with <py>w</py>.

Also, in English _ou_ sometimes sounds like <egpy>ao</egpy>, such
as in the word <eg src="cloud">cloud</eg>.  However, remember that in
_pinyin_, there are two distinct sounds that are always spelled
in two distinct ways: <egpy>ao</egpy> and <egpy>ou</egpy>.
