## The Basic Outline

In this tutorial, you will learn the finals first.  You will practice
hearing and saying each final in each of the four tones.

Because there are so few syllables in Mandarin, it is possible to list
_all_ the finals and teach each one.  That means that if you want
to spell something in a way you haven't been taught, you know you must
be wrong.

For example, there is no final spelled _ay_.  (You can look at
the table of contents at left and see that this is so.)  Thus, if you
hear a sound such as <egpy>bei</egpy> (click to hear), you know that
it can't be spelled _bay_.

In the beginning, every time you want to spell something, you should
ask yourself if you've seen this spelling before.  If you haven't, you
should think through what you've learned so far and try something
else.  Eventually this process will become automatic, and misspelled
_pinyin_ will jump out at you just as misspelled English does.

Once you get the finals down, you will go on to the initials.  We do
it in this order because some of the finals are spelled differently,
depending on whether or not the syllable has an initial.

This tutorial software is _interactive_.  You will be required to
read, to listen, and to type.  The next few pages will familiarize
you with the buttons and the blanks you will encounter.

## Listening

<listen syl="bīng"></listen>
<listen syl="lǒu" icon></listen>

Buttons like the ones above will allow you to hear various
_pinyin_ syllables.  Sometimes the syllable will be displayed, as
in the button on top.  At other times, the syllable will be
hidden from you, as in the button on the bottom; this often occurs when
you are practicing your listening skills.

Click on each of the buttons and see what you hear.

Sometimes a sentence will have an underlined English word like
<eg src="this">this</eg>, or an underlined _pinyin_ syllable like
<egpy>piáo</egpy>.  If you click on anything underlined, you will hear
it pronounced.  This will give you a chance to compare English and
Chinese pronunciation.

Sometimes a sentence will appear next to a speaker button.  Click on
the speaker button to hear it.

## Typing

<syllableentry></syllableentry>

Boxes like the one above are for typing in _pinyin_.  You type
the letters just like you usually would.  To enter a tone mark, place
the cursor immediately after the vowel you want to put the mark over,
and then type one of the numbers 1 to 4.  To clear a tone mark, type
0, or just delete the vowel and type it in again.

Try entering the following syllables.  The number of the tone is
given in parentheses:

<py>bā</py> (1) <py>lín</py> (2) <py>chǒng</py> (3) <py>mèi</py> (4)

## Hearing What You Type

<syllableplayer><syllableplayer>

A box with a speaker button lets you hear what you've typed.

Try entering the same syllables as last time, but this time click on
the speaker button afterwards to hear what they sound like.  The
number of the tone is given in parentheses:

<py>bā</py> (1) <py>lín</py> (2) <py>chǒng</py> (3) <py>mèi</py> (4)

If you enter a syllable that doesn't exist in _pinyin_, you will
get a warning and an explanation when you click on the speaker button.

Try entering the following "illegal" syllables into the box above and
see what messages you get.  As before, the number of the tone is given
in parentheses:

<py>bāu</py> (1) <py>líen</py> (2) <py>chiǒng</py> (3) <py>mèy</py> (4)

You can try fixing your mistakes and hearing the results if you wish.

If you ever want to have more boxes that you can type syllables in so
you can hear them pronounced, click on the button "Hear a Syllable."
A small window will pop up for you.  You can pop up as many of these
windows as you like and place them anywhere you want on the screen.
